myths and mysticisms of the mind

Intro

We live in a time where people have already been on the moon, sequenced the entire human genome, and split an atom. It’s easy to believe that there's not much left to be discovered. On the contrary, there are still many things we don’t fully understand that we thought we did, and one of those things is inside of your skull: it’s your brain.

Today I want to debunk myths about the brain's form and functions, and also share some of its lesser known abilities.

Metaphysical myths

To start, I'd like to go over some commonly shared myths about the brain. The first and most egregious being that we only use 10% of our brains. AKA the tagline for a 2014 sci-fi movie "Lucy" ft. Scarlett Johansson. It is simply wrong. We use all parts of our brain. There is some localization, so different parts may be activated for different activities, but the statement that we only use 10% is false. Second, unless you had a brain hemisphere removed or are a genetic anomaly born with only half of your brain, there are no left-brained versus right-brained people. Although there is no harm in using it as a colloquial shortcut to communicate a person's interests or tendencies, no one can tell the difference between the brain of a mathematician or an artist by looking at an MRI, CAT scan, or autopsy. These are the common myths shared about the brain's physical usage.

Metacognitive myths

Next, I want to debunk some metacognitive beliefs about the brain. One being, the word "multi-tasking". Your brain can't actually multi-task. What you're perceiving as multi-tasking is the brain switching between tasks and doing them all poorly. It is less efficient and worsens your ability to focus. In fact, switching between different contexts can waste as much as 20 minutes for your brain to resume the previously interrupted activity. Second, people say that willpower is finite, but this was recently disproven in the past year. In fact, believing that your willpower is finite impacts your future willpower. But believing that it is a renewable resource will help you to have more willpower. It’s incredible how much you can change your brain’s capacity just by changing how you think about it.

Mysticisms

Finally, I want to share interesting things your brain can do that seem mystical, and dare I say, magical. The first is one you are probably familiar with--the placebo effect--which is when people experience a beneficial health outcome from a fake treatment or "placebo" pill, thinking it is an actual pill. But did you know that the placebo effect can even work when people know and are told it is a placebo? Known as the open-label placebo effect, this phenomenon exhibits how much our beliefs can impact not just our mental state but even our physical state. In a similar vein of illusory deception, a mirror trick can be used as physical therapy for amputees. Patients with phantom limb syndrome, where they have pain in their amputated limb, are therapized using mirrors to replace where their missing limb is, to help build the new mind-body connection to relieve the pain. Another mystical cognitive tool that helps relieve pain is meditation. A UW study found that even beginners to meditation can curb pain after just four 20-minute training sessions. These are some of many mystical phenomena of your mind.

Conclusion

To conclude, there are many myths about the mind and how it works that have since been disproven. Many of which are self-limiting. Luckily, there are also many mysticisms of the brain that empower us that have been discovered and are left to be discovered. Going forth, I hope you can keep an open mind about your mind, as it's probably capable of much more than you know.

Thank you.
