Ruminations and remnants / toasting for my past, present, and future selves

I joined Toastmasters and this was my first speech. The project is called an "Icebreaker Speech".

toasting for my past, present, and future selves

My name is Yoonsie and I’m here today because I sound like Eric Cartman from South Park. When I’m not consciously trying to avoid it, I enunciate my words like he does. It has nothing to do with my race or being a child of immigrants since this was a “problem” unique to me and not my siblings, but it has everything to do with my tongue. I figured this out as an adult, when a dentist told me that my tongue is too big for my narrow mouth so it’s constantly touching my teeth. Well actually, he told it to the student he was training, not to me. He also told the student to stop leaving your tools on the patient, they’re not tables.

I’ve never liked the way that I sound. When I was a kid I sounded like a chipmunk with a lisp. That should have been the first sign that my tongue was too big for my mouth, since I had an interdental lisp and said “s”’s and “z’s”. Basically I used to sound like Sylvester the Cat before I evolved to Eric Cartman. So, when I first heard of Toastmasters as a teenager, I thought to myself, I will join this club when I am an adult so that I can finally learn to enunciate better, even when I’m not consciously trying, the same way I got rid of my lisp as a kid and no longer have to think about my s’s and z’s.

That was the initial reason from my past for joining Toastmasters. But as I got older and, I like to think, wizened by the years, I became a fan of the belief that we don’t all have to sound alike. After all, movies like Rocky and The Terminator are iconic for their leading character’s unique speech. But, I have a reason from the present for joining Toastmasters. And this time it doesn’t have to do with my tongue, it has to do with my brain.

I’m an overthinker with thoughts that often get messy and overwhelming. Public speaking will get me to write more. And writing more will get me to organize my thoughts more. And organizing my thoughts more get me to practice the process of detangling my thoughts into something more cohesive that I can hopefully successfully communicate to other people.

Which brings me to my last and most important reason for joining—one that is rooted in my hopes for the future and hopefully my community’s future. We communicate to connect and understand each other, and in a modern society that is becoming more disconnected and in a country that is becoming more divided, I want to communicate my thoughts with more clarity, to listen to others more actively, and to echo their thoughts back to them with more understanding and compassion. At the end of the day, I strongly believe that people just want to feel seen and heard. And I think that’s the reason why I’m here. So I can learn to express myself in a way to feel seen and heard while also learning to communicate with others in a way to help them feel seen and heard. So this is a toast for my past, present, and future selves and the reasons I’ve joined this club.

Thank you
